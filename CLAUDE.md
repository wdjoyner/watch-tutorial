# Notes for AI-assisted editing

Read this first. Read code only as needed. For a SETTINGS or voice-text change, open just that block or file.

## Files
- `movement.py`: shared watch model, based on the ETA 6497-1 (hand-wound, 18,000 vph, no rotor). Palette and fonts, tooth counts (`TEETH`), pivot layout (`P`), `state(t)` kinematics, geometry helpers (`vm`, `slab`, `wheel_g`, `teeth_g`, `stripes_g`, …), the `Movement` class (tiers `dial` / `engine` / `top`, `parts` dict, `rot` list), `attach_driver()`.
- `films/filmNN_<name>/`: one folder per film. Film 1 is `film01_movement_architecture`; Film 2 (mainspring and barrel) is `film02_power_source`; Film 3 (the going train) is `film03_gear_train`; Film 4 (the balance and hairspring) is `film04_regulating_organ`. Film 5 (isochronism) is `film05_isochronism`. Film 6 (the escapement) is `film06_escapement`.
  - `sceneNN_*.py`: one Manim scene per file. `SETTINGS` block at the top, then the timeline. Scenes import from the shared `movement.py` (build.py puts the repo root on `PYTHONPATH`).
  - `sceneNN_*.voice.txt`: narration, `start_seconds | sentence`.
  - `running_order.py`: `TITLE`, `OUT_NAME`, and `SCENES` (file, chapter name) for that film.
  - Film-local helper modules may sit beside the scenes (build.py also puts the film folder on `PYTHONPATH`). Film 2's `barrel.py` is the close-up mainspring barrel (`BarrelModel`: trackers `explode`, `lift`, `drum_turn`, `arbor_turn`, `show_cover`; `anchor()` gives points on parts for labels). Its `pack_state_g()` draws the spring as it winds at real 6497 numbers (arbor pack, wall pack, free coil; about 8 turns of development).
  - Film 3's `train.py`: flat gears that mesh in phase (`GearSet`: `gear(name, z, m, center= | driven_by=, angle= | arbor_of=)`, one updater turns all from `gs.turn` / `gs.speed`; fade gears with `gs.show()` / `gear.vis`, never FadeIn, which fights the rotation) and `elevation_arbor()` side views. Film 3 scenes call `movement.use_train(movement.TEETH_6497)` before building, for the documented 6497 counts (85/12, 80/10, 60/8, 70/7, 15); the defaults in `TEETH` stay as Films 1 and 2 were rendered.
  - Film 4's `figures.py`: every number Film 4 shows or says, computed from its inputs with its source (`python figures.py` prints them). Values ETA doesn't publish (balance inertia, hairspring size, amplitude, Q) are the chapter's representative ones and must be labeled so on screen. For slow motion, Film 4 passes `rate=` (a ValueTracker, 1 = real time) to `attach_driver`; without it the clock runs exactly as before.
  - Film 4's `balance.py`: `BalanceModel`, a flat balance whose hairspring breathes (inner end turns with the staff, outer end held at the stud or curb pins); animate its trackers `rate`, `amp`, `damp`, `drive`/`manual`, `vis`, `pins`, never the balance itself. `BeatLog` records each beat's video time (from a model clock, or from `rate=`) and `log.write(__file__)` writes `<scene>.ticks.txt`. Also `escape_wheel_g()`. Film 4's `ui.py`: `ftext` (formulas as Text), `label`, `heading`, `tag`, `sci`, `m` (minus signs), `faded` (fade redrawn paths; `set_opacity` would fill lines), `clock` (wait_until). Write sub/superscripts as Unicode (ω₀, αₛ, 10⁻⁹); `ftext`/`label`/`tag` set them with Pango `<sub>`/`<sup>`, because the macOS fonts lack those glyphs (`ui.text()` for other Text).
  - Film 4 traps: never `.animate` anything inside a moving `BalanceModel` (it snapshots the points and fights the swing; drive opacity with a tracker and an updater); in a 3D scene's fixed layer, update persistent mobjects instead of `always_redraw` (its new parts are not registered as fixed).
  - Film 5 reuses Film 4's `balance.py` and `ui.py` from Film 4's folder: a Film 5 scene does `import iso` (or `import f4`) first, which appends Film 4's folder to `sys.path` (Film 5's own `figures.py` still wins; Film 4's numbers are `f4.figures4`). Film 5's `figures.py` holds its numbers, including a two-arc terminal curve solved to meet Phillips' conditions (`python figures.py` prints the check). Its `iso.py`: `IsoBalance` (a `BalanceModel` whose frequency follows f(Θ) = f₀(1 + 3βΘ²/8κ); trackers `beta`, and `offset` for beat error; `real_amp()`), `CrossLog` (ticks at each crossing of the line of centers, for beat error; writes `<scene>.ticks.txt`), `Plot` (a plain plot frame, no LaTeX), `FlatSpring` (a schematic breathing spiral with `cg()`).
  - Film 6 reuses Films 4 and 5's helpers from their folders: a Film 6 scene does `import prev` first, which appends Film 5's then Film 4's folder to `sys.path` (Film 6's own `figures.py` still wins; theirs are `prev.figures4`, `prev.figures5`). No shared file changes. Film 6's `figures.py` holds its numbers with sources (the chapter, Petrangeli 2017, ETA CT 6497-1's 44 deg lift angle, Seiko patent US 6,431,746 FIG. 8 for the torque curve). Its `lever.py`: `LeverEscapement`, a 2D Swiss lever close-up built from the chapter figure's outlines (`lever_geometry.json`); the wheel and lever follow the balance by contact, from `lever_table.json` (rebuild with `python lever.py`, ~5 s). Animate its trackers `rate`, `lift_rate` (extra slow-down near the center), `amp`, `drive`/`manual` (psi in degrees), `vis`, `rim`, never the parts; `anchor(name)` for labels, `phase()`, and `events` (video times of unlock / impulse / drop / bank) for sound effects. Film 6 layout rule: keep text and figures above y = -2.7 (the captions sit below); check with a probe that records each visible piece's bottom after every play. `running_order.SHORT_CAPTIONS = True` makes `build.py` split captions into two-line cues (opt-in; Films 1-5 unchanged). Film 6 notation: phi angle, Theta amplitude (never A), Theta* settled amplitude, phi' angular speed, t_k and phi_k (time and angle of a kick; never phi*), J kick size; `scene03b_symbols.py` introduces them.
  - `sceneNN_*.ticks.txt` (optional, written by slow-motion scenes on each render): the video time of each beat; build.py uses it in place of the steady 5-beats-a-second tick track. Scenes without one sound as before.
  - `sceneNN_*.sfx.txt` (optional): sound effects, `time | click [| level]`, mixed in by build.py (`--sfx-level`); sounds are `click`, and `wood` / `bell` (two pitched ticks, used by Film 5 scene 07's listening demo). Film 2 scene 03 and Film 5 scene 07 write their own on every render.
- `films.py`: resolves a film (`2`, `film02`, or folder name) and a scene (`scene03_x`, or `2/scene00_title` when the name occurs in several films).
- `build.py`: render → Kokoro TTS (cached per sentence) → tick track → .srt → `out/<film>/<scene>.mp4`. Manim media goes to `media/<film>/`. Its `PRONOUNCE` table respells words Kokoro gets wrong, for the voice only ("wound" → "wownd", "wind" → "wined"); add entries there, not in the .voice.txt files. If the narration runs past the animation, build.py warns and holds the last frame; fix it by lengthening the scene's final hold, so the narration ends before the fade-out.
- `film.py <film>`: joins that film's built scenes in running order into `out/<OUT_NAME>.mp4` with merged subtitles and chapters; no re-encoding. Each film's `scene00_title.py` and last scene are the title and credits cards (no narration).

## Rules that matter
- Never `.animate` a tier or a rotating part, because that suspends the driver updater. Animate the trackers returned by `attach_driver` instead (`explode`, `dim[tier]`), or the camera.
- Add parts bottom-to-top within a tier (painter's algorithm). Use `lift(m, dz)` for small z offsets inside a tier.
- A new rotating part needs an entry in `Movement.rot`: `(mobject, pivot_xy, tier, lambda s: angle)`. Derive its angle from `state()` so speeds stay consistent with the gear ratios.
- A train wheel's center distance must equal the sum of the pitch radii. Each going-train mesh has its own module (`MESH`), chosen so the traced 6497 pivot positions mesh exactly; use `R[...]` for radii.
- The dial tier is built dial side up (its own frame: positions mirrored with `dv()`, dial face at z = 0) and fitted dial side down under the plate. Animate `mv.flip` (0 -> 1) to turn it over; `Movement(dial_up=True)` starts it turned. Dial-tier `rot` pivots and angles are in that dial-up frame; `mv.world(tier, xy, z)` converts to world coordinates.
- Winding and regulating: animate `mv.wind` (crown turns) and `mv.regulate` (degrees), never the parts.
- Screen-fixed text uses `add_fixed_in_frame_mobjects`.
- Timed cues during a camera move: build the move with `camera_move()` and wrap each cue in `cue(when, ..., start=, end=)`, both in `movement.py`. `move_camera(added_anims=...)` passes its run_time and rate_func to every added animation, which stretches the cues.
- To center a 3D point on screen, use `camera_move(..., target=p, frame_origin=<the scene's starting frame_center>)`. Manim's Cairo 3D camera shifts the picture by the starting frame_center, so a plain `frame_center=p` lands off-center.
- With a nonzero starting frame_center, the Cairo 3D camera also shifts screen-fixed mobjects by -frame_center (and the 3D picture by about twice that). Film 3 scene 07 shifts its fixed layer back by +frame_center.
- Check framing in a real `-q l` video, not a `-s` still: a still only renders the last frame, so it hides the frame_center offset.
- Don't render several scenes in parallel: they share Manim's text cache and can corrupt it (fix: `find media -path "*texts*" -size 0 -delete`).
- Fonts: Helvetica Neue / Georgia on macOS, DejaVu elsewhere (set in `movement.py`).
- Use American English spelling in all text, comments and docstrings.

## Efficient workflow
- The user renders final videos on a Mac Studio with `python build.py <scene>` (or `<film>/<scene>`) and `python film.py <film>`.
- For a quick check in the cloud workspace, run `python build.py <film>/<scene> -q l` and inspect only a few stills (`ffmpeg -ss T -i out/<film>/<scene>.mp4 -frames:v 1 x.png`). Skip stills for trivial changes.
- Prefer targeted edits over rewriting files. Batch the user's notes into one pass.
- In the cloud workspace, installing Manim needs `apt-get install libcairo2-dev libpango1.0-dev pkg-config`. If the `srt` package fails to build, install it from its sdist by copying `srt.py` into site-packages.
