1.0  | How do you measure the amplitude without seeing the balance? Listen.
6.1  | The roller jewel touches the fork only while the balance crosses a small arc around the midpoint of its swing, called the lift angle. ETA gives forty-four degrees for the sixty-four ninety-seven.
20.2 | Slowed right down, a tick has three parts: the unlock, the impulse, and the drop.
29.1 | The machine times the gap from unlock to drop. At two hundred seventy degrees, it's about ten milliseconds.
37.4 | A big swing crosses the lift arc quickly, and a small one slowly, so the gap gives the amplitude.
45.1 | Enter the wrong lift angle, say fifty-two degrees, and the same watch reads three hundred nineteen. The rate is still right, because it comes from the spacing of the ticks, not what happens inside them.
